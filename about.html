About Pollards Inn

On the green since 1637

A village inn with nearly four centuries behind it.

We're a village pub on the green in Willaston, cooking proper food and pouring a proper pint. Pies are made in our own kitchen, Sundays mean a roast with all the trimmings, and everyone gets the same warm welcome.

The building is Grade II listed: a red sandstone manor house built in 1637. Inside, the beams and ceiling joists are exposed, and a huge stone chimney stack still runs the full height of the house.

Four centuries on the green

From manor house to local.

Pollards Inn began life in 1637 as a manor house, built of red sandstone on Willaston's village green. It was altered and extended around 1700, and later became a farmhouse known as Corner House Farm. In the 1980s it was converted and opened as a pub.

Willaston is recorded in the Domesday Book, and its green may once have been the meeting place for the whole of Wirral. Pollards sits among a cluster of 17th-century buildings around it, including The Old Red Lion from 1631. The Grade II listing protects the fabric of the house; the feeling of the place comes from the people in it.
